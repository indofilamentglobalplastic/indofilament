Floor Brush Filament: How to Choose the Right Diameter and Stiffness

Published 2026-11-11 · PT Indofilament Global Plastic

For a brush manufacturer or brand owner, the filament is the part of a floor brush that customers judge first, even if they never say so. A brush that is too soft will not lift dirt from grout lines. A brush that is too stiff can scratch glossy tiles and feel tiring to use. Getting floor brush filament right comes down to two main choices: diameter and stiffness.

Why diameter and stiffness matter

Every floor brush is a compromise between cleaning power and surface safety. The filament decides where that balance sits. Diameter, length and stiffness work together, so changing one affects how the others behave. A good specification starts from the end use, not from whatever filament happens to be in stock.

Diameter: the starting point

Diameter is the thickness of each individual filament strand. As a general rule:

  • Thinner filaments feel softer, flex more easily, and pick up fine dust and light dirt. They suit smooth, delicate surfaces.
  • Thicker filaments are firmer and stronger, and they push harder against stuck-on dirt. They suit rough floors and heavy scrubbing.

Diameter also affects the look of the brush head. Thin filaments give a denser, fuller appearance at the same bundle size, while thick filaments give a more open, robust look. Both can be intentional product positioning choices.

Stiffness: more than just thickness

Stiffness is how strongly the filament resists bending. Diameter is the biggest factor, but length also matters: a longer filament of the same diameter bends more easily than a short one. This is why two brushes with the same filament can feel completely different when the trim length changes.

Stiffness should also be consistent. If strands in the same brush vary in firmness, the brush wears unevenly and the bristles splay out early. For brands producing thousands of units, consistent filament from batch to batch is as important as the specification itself.

Matching filament to the surface

These are practical starting points for product planning:

  • Smooth ceramic, glossy tile and polished floors: choose medium to softer filament to reduce the risk of scratching while still lifting everyday dirt.
  • Textured tile, bathroom floors and grout lines: choose firmer filament so the tips can reach into the texture.
  • Outdoor floors, terraces and rough concrete: choose thicker, stiffer filament that holds its shape under pressure.
  • Wet areas: nylon filament is a good fit because it keeps its function in frequent contact with water and cleaning liquids.

Many brands use a tiered range: a soft version for everyday home use, a medium version as the main product, and a firm version for heavy-duty cleaning. This lets one brush design serve several customer needs by changing only the filament.

Length and bundle density

Filament length determines how much the bristle flexes under pressure. Longer filament feels more flexible and reaches uneven areas. Shorter filament gives more direct scrubbing force. Combine this with bundle density: a dense head with firm filament gives strong scrubbing, while a lighter bundle with soft filament feels gentler. Specify diameter, length and density together rather than separately.

Color as part of the product

Color is not a technical factor, but it affects shelf appeal and product coding. Our standard colors are yellow, green, blue and pink, plus the Gold Series (Bright Gold and Copper Gold with a pearl finish) for brands that want a premium look. Red, purple, orange, black, white and custom colors can be ordered. Some brands use color to separate soft, medium and firm versions in the same range.

How to test before ordering in volume

  1. Define the target surface and the target customer.
  2. Request samples in two or three diameter and stiffness options.
  3. Fit them to your actual brush bodies, not just evaluate them loose.
  4. Test on real floors with real dirt, and compare scrubbing power, scratch risk, and hand feel.
  5. Check how the bristles look after repeated use and washing.
  6. Confirm the final specification, then place the production order.

Is a stiffer filament always better for cleaning floors?

No. Stiffer filament scrubs harder on rough or textured floors, but it can scratch glossy surfaces and feel tiring to use. The best choice depends on the floor type and the customer you are targeting, which is why many brands offer soft, medium and firm versions.
